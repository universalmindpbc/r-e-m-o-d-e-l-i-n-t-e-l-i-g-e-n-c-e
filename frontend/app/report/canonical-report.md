# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## The decision in one sentence

Rihanna should not invent a new climate proposition. She should make CLF's established model of community-led Caribbean resilience visible at cultural scale, then give people a credible pathway from attention to understanding, action, return and leadership.

The opportunity begins with an unusually strong foundation. CLF has a documented climate-solutions portfolio, a multi-year record of flexible funding and infrastructure, and a founder whose Caribbean identity is integral rather than incidental to the cause. Between 2021 and 2025, CLF reports more than $27 million deployed to 39 climate-resilience partners and more than 2.8 million people reached. Its current Climate Solutions page separately reports $20.6 million invested across the Caribbean and U.S. South. Those figures describe different portfolio views and should not be combined; together, they nevertheless establish that this is sustained operating work, not a one-off association.

The central strategic problem is not a lack of credibility. It is a gap between **proof and participation**. Climate justice is expressed through systems that are hard to compress: electricity that stays on, water that remains available, a school designed to become a shelter, a mobile clinic capable of reaching people across an island state, capital controlled closer to communities, and institutions that can continue operating through shocks. Celebrity culture, by contrast, is engineered for immediacy, spectacle and repetition. Without a designed bridge, the work can remain institutionally meaningful yet culturally isolated.

This report recommends building that bridge around CLF's existing **Rebuild & Reimagine** platform. The public proposition is simple: **Built by communities. Backed by culture. Measured in resilience.** The platform would organise proof, community voice, cultural translation, verified action and public accountability into a year-long system. Rihanna's role is not to speak for frontline communities or become the sole protagonist. Her role is to make verified work difficult to ignore, direct attention and resources toward community-defined priorities, and return publicly to what changed.

The proposed **Commercial-to-Climate Pipeline** meets audiences inside behaviours that already exist—music, fashion, beauty, loyalty, sharing and fandom—then redesigns those touchpoints to create learning, partner discovery, action and repeat participation. Four moves make the pipeline credible: make proof legible; build a return pathway; commission culture as infrastructure; and gate every commercial claim behind auditable evidence and community authority. Concepts such as Fenty Trace, climate-ready Caribbean design and participatory micro-grants are recommended strategic scenarios, not announced programmes.

Measurement is deliberately separated into three different truths. **Talent / CLF** measures delivered evidence, founder–cause authenticity and narrative coherence. **Audience** measures what defined cohorts know, understand, trust and do. **Target** defines the agreed future state and the evidence thresholds required to claim progress. Public evidence supports a strong qualitative Talent / CLF position. The audience position is currently unmeasured. The target is a strategic scenario. No responsible report can turn followers, likes or synthetic examples into a defensible audience score.

The first 90 days therefore combine evidence governance with audience research. CLF and delivery partners would validate proof stories, project baselines, narrative consent and priority outcomes. Authorised owned-channel data, a permission-based survey, stakeholder interviews and a licensed listening pilot—only where it closes a named evidence gap—would establish the baseline. Digital-twin modelling may then stress-test messages and content routes before live investment, but synthetic outputs remain decision support and must be validated with real people.

The near-term decision is whether to authorise a Year One design phase around Rebuild & Reimagine. Approval should be conditional on five commitments: community authority over story and programme relevance; a baseline before numeric audience claims; a public evidence dictionary; a commercial-claim gate; and a year-end accountability moment that reports both progress and unresolved gaps. If those conditions hold, Rihanna can occupy a distinctive global position: not celebrity environmentalism in the abstract, but culture in service of community-led resilience.

### Eight findings that shape the strategy

1. **The cause fit is already credible.** Rihanna's Caribbean identity and CLF's sustained portfolio create an authentic foundation that should be clarified rather than reinvented.
2. **The work is practical and systemic at the same time.** Infrastructure, health, water, education, local finance and justice are connected parts of resilience.
3. **The strongest story is not disaster spectacle.** It is everyday continuity: communities able to live, learn, heal and lead before, during and after a shock.
4. **Public proof is dispersed.** The evidence exists across reports, projects and moments but is not yet organised as one persistent cultural journey.
5. **Audience understanding is unknown.** The attention-deficit thesis is strategically plausible, but the 95:5, 12× and 92% examples are synthetic hypotheses—not findings.
6. **Culture can increase efficacy when the action is specific.** Research suggests that collective-efficacy information and strong parasocial ties can improve action intentions; celebrity presence without an actionable pathway is insufficient.
7. **The dashboard must preserve provenance.** Delivery, audience response and strategic destination should never collapse into one unexplained number.
8. **Community power is the credibility test.** A programme is not climate justice if culture or commerce recentres the celebrity and weakens local authority.

# 01 / THE DECISION

## Rihanna does not need to manufacture a climate-justice position. Through CLF, she already funds one.

The strategic opportunity is not to invent a cause. It is to make an established body of community-led work more legible, participatory and accountable at cultural scale.

Between 2021 and 2025, CLF reports deploying more than $27 million to 39 climate-resilience partners and reaching more than 2.8 million people through clean energy, health systems, environmental justice and community organising. Its current Climate Solutions page reports $20.6 million invested specifically in climate solutions across the Caribbean and U.S. South. The foundation’s latest operating model visibly joins long-term adaptation, emergency response, local leadership and strategic partnership. [CLF 2025 Annual Report](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/2025report); [CLF Climate Solutions](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/our-works/climate-solutions)

These are parallel CLF-reported portfolio views with different apparent scopes and periods. They must not be added or treated as reconciled. The separate 5.2 million figure in CLF's 2025 report is organisation-wide 2025 reach, not a climate-only figure.

CLF provides documented delivery credibility. Rihanna's global cultural reach is a strategic premise in this public-evidence edition; it is not quantified here. Our directional diagnosis is that the public evidence and the audience-action pathway are not yet joined into one measurable system: a narrative and action layer that helps audiences understand what climate justice means, see what it looks like in practice, take a meaningful role, and return to act again.

### Executive thesis

**Build one visible, annual platform around CLF’s existing “Rebuild & Reimagine” work. Use culture to convert attention into repeat action, while Caribbean communities retain decision power. Measure three different things separately: what CLF and partners deliver, what audiences understand and do, and what wider capital or policy the cultural platform helps mobilise.**

### The recommended territory

**REBUILD & REIMAGINE**  
**Built by communities. Backed by culture. Measured in resilience.**

This is not a new foundation proposition. It is a cultural and measurement layer for CLF’s existing Rebuild & Reimagine Fund, which currently focuses on health access, water security and resilient education infrastructure. [CLF 2025 Annual Report](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/2025report)

### The ambition

Move from:

- significant work that is credible but unevenly visible;
- high-profile moments without a persistent audience pathway;
- aggregate impact reporting without a shared cultural scorecard;

to:

- a recognisable Rihanna × climate-justice position;
- a year-long ladder from attention to learning, action and repeat participation;
- partner-owned proof of resilience, justice and mobilisation;
- one annual cultural moment that publishes receipts, renews commitments and compounds action.

# 03 / WHY CLIMATE JUSTICE, WHY THE CARIBBEAN, WHY NOW

Climate justice is not simply climate communication with a social theme. It asks who created the least pollution, who carries the greatest risk, who controls finance and decisions, and who benefits from adaptation.

Small Island Developing States are among the world’s most exposed countries. The World Bank records 185 tropical-cyclone disasters across Caribbean SIDS from 1995 to 2022, causing $45 billion in damage in 2022 prices. In some island states, annual public investment requirements for adaptation are measured in material shares of GDP. [World Bank: SIDS by the numbers](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/climatechange/four-things-you-should-know--climate-change---small-island-devel)

The financing imbalance is structural. UN Trade and Development reported that aid to SIDS fell 13% to $5.9 billion in 2022, while climate-related aid fell 23% to $1.5 billion. Natural-hazard damage in the Caribbean is estimated at $12.6 billion a year, and loan-heavy finance risks deepening a debt–climate trap. [United Nations in the Caribbean](https://caribbean.un.org/en/270450-aid-small-islands-falls-even-temperatures-rise)

One storm can overwhelm national finances. The World Bank reports that Hurricane Beryl caused damage equivalent to more than 20% of GDP in St. Vincent and the Grenadines; rapid-access financing and debt clauses were needed to create recovery space. [World Bank Crisis Toolkit](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2025/04/28/how-the-world-bank-s-crisis-toolkit-is-empowering-caribbean-small-states)

## Implication

The credible Rihanna position is not “save the planet.” It is more precise:

> Communities that contributed least to the crisis should have the power, infrastructure and finance to live, learn, heal and lead through it.

That position is globally relevant and distinctly grounded in Rihanna’s Caribbean identity and CLF’s operating history.

# 07 / THE OPPORTUNITY GAP

## The gap is not “Rihanna versus climate justice.” It is “verified work versus understood and repeated action.”

### The structural paradox

CLF champions a progressive, intersectional and trust-based model of climate justice and hurricane resilience. Rihanna's wider cultural ecosystem, by contrast, is built to reward immediacy: music anticipation, beauty and fashion desire, spectacle, memes and product conversation. The risk is that complex foundation work remains institutionally credible but culturally isolated from the people most attached to its founder.

That is a **strategic inference**, not a measured verdict on Rihanna's audience. The 95:5, 12x and 92% demonstrations dramatise the possible shape of the problem; they do not prove it. Re-Model's job is to test whether the paradox exists, determine where it is strongest, and design the bridge if it does.

### The compression problem

Structural policy work does not naturally compress into a viral loop. A school that doubles as a hurricane shelter, a mobile clinic that extends resilient healthcare or flexible funding that shifts decision power all require context. If the work is communicated only as an announcement, attention may spike and disappear without producing comprehension or action.

The answer is not to trivialise policy. It is to create cultural forms that preserve meaning while making the issue easier to enter, remember and act on.

### What the evidence says about celebrity, efficacy and action

Celebrity attention is not a guaranteed bridge to public action. Research on celebrity advocacy repeatedly distinguishes **visibility** from **engagement**: a familiar figure can make an issue easier to notice, yet attention may remain attached to the celebrity rather than the underlying system. That is particularly important for climate justice, where the meaningful unit of change is rarely an individual consumer choice alone. The creative task is to use familiarity as an entrance while making community actors, collective efficacy and a specific action pathway progressively more visible.

A recent programme of experiments and field research involving 2,960 participants found that climate messages containing collective-efficacy information increased efficacy and action intentions, with stronger effects among people reporting stronger parasocial relationships with the influencer. Repeated exposure reinforced parts of that pathway. The useful implication is not that Rihanna's audience will behave identically; it is that influence becomes more actionable when a message explains what people can do together, why the action matters and how it connects to a real outcome. [Human Communication Research](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article/52/3/147/8300321)

This supports a disciplined role for Rihanna. She can lower the cultural cost of entry, confer attention and make repeated participation desirable. She cannot substitute celebrity identification for issue comprehension or community authority. Each cultural moment should therefore answer four questions in sequence: **What is happening? Who is already leading? What can I do that is proportionate and verifiable? When will I learn what happened next?**

The strategy must also allow for refusal, critique and unequal resonance. Some audiences may distrust celebrity advocacy, reject a commercial pathway or regard global fandom as a distraction from climate finance and policy. Those are not noise to be optimised away. They are inputs into the authenticity and governance tests, and they should remain visible in qualitative analysis rather than being flattened into a favourable sentiment score.

### Gap 1 — Visibility

CLF has credible receipts, but they are not yet organised into one persistent public evidence journey.

### Gap 2 — Comprehension

Climate justice can remain abstract unless it is shown through real schools, clinics, water systems, local leadership and days of service preserved.

### Gap 3 — Participation

Audiences need more than a donation button. They need actions appropriate to their location, capability and stage of commitment.

### Gap 4 — Accountability

Reach, engagement, grants and people reached do not by themselves show whether basic services remained available through shocks or communities gained decision power.

### Gap 5 — Compounding

An annual halo event should not be a one-night endpoint. It should be the public checkpoint of a year-long programme and the launch of the next cycle.

## Re-Model's antidote: the Commercial-to-Climate Pipeline

The pipeline meets audiences inside behaviours that already exist—watching, listening, shopping, sharing, loyalty and fandom—and redesigns those touchpoints to create a route into verified climate learning, community-defined action and repeat participation.

| Strategic initiative | Expanded operating concept | Evidence and justice gate | Intended growth mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fenty Trace** | A digital evidence layer connected to selected Fenty touchpoints, showing material and supply-chain claims, the climate-justice issue they relate to, the partner mechanism and a next action | Launch only where product and supply-chain data are auditable; avoid generic offset education or implied whole-brand sustainability claims | Turns product attention into specific learning and evidence-seeking behaviour |
| **Climate-Ready Caribbean Design** | Limited, high-design collaborations with Caribbean designers and resilience practitioners, with a defined funding mechanism for partner-approved infrastructure or preparedness priorities | Publish the beneficiary, net contribution, rights, production footprint and decision authority; “100% of profits” only if contractually true and independently reconcilable | Meets fashion interest without treating purchase as sufficient action; creates visibility, income and cultural authorship |
| **Participatory Micro-Grant Pathway** | Loyalty members or fans learn about eligible partner-defined projects, complete a meaningful action and help allocate a ring-fenced incremental pool among pre-approved options | CLF and frontline partners set eligibility and retain final authority; participation adds capital rather than overruling existing grants | Converts loyalty into partner discovery, informed participation and return behaviour |
| **Proof-to-Action Releases** | Treat selected music, fashion or cultural moments as timed gateways into a partner story, evidence card and action ladder | Every release has named sources, consent, a destination and a post-moment results update | Uses cultural peaks to build a persistent action habit rather than an isolated spike |

### Pipeline logic

**Familiar behaviour → climate proof → community voice → verified action → partner update → repeat action**

This is the core design response to the hypothesised attention deficit. It connects commercial and cultural attention to climate justice without claiming that consumption itself is impact.

### Pipeline decision gates

The pipeline is not a content funnel in which reach is assumed to become impact. Each transition is a management decision with a minimum proof condition. **Entry** asks whether the cultural or commercial moment is relevant enough to earn attention without distorting the issue. **Translation** asks whether the climate proof is specific, accessible and approved by the people whose work is being represented. **Action** asks whether a participant can do something proportionate and verifiable beyond passive engagement. **Return** asks whether the programme can report what happened, what remains unresolved and what the audience should do next.

Every proposed activation should therefore carry a one-page pathway brief before approval: the originating audience behaviour; the named partner and community benefit; the evidence available at launch; the intended audience action; the data that may ethically be collected; the follow-up commitment; and the accountable owner. If any link is absent, the activation remains an awareness output rather than a Rebuild & Reimagine pathway.

This discipline protects the strategy from two common failures. The first is **attention leakage**, where a large cultural moment generates views but leaves no durable route into knowledge or action. The second is **impact substitution**, where a commercial transaction, post or pledge is reported as though it were community resilience. The dashboard should expose both failures: it should show movement between stages, not merely activity at the top of the funnel, and it should never convert an audience signal into an outcome claim without separate programme evidence.

REVERB reports that Billie Eilish’s Hit Me Hard and Soft tour raised $13.3 million for climate and community organisations, counted 215,117 heterogeneous fan actions, hosted more than 160 nonprofits and estimates that programme measures avoided the potential use of more than 135,260 single-use bottles. The model embeds action villages, ticket-linked funding, partner presence and venue operating standards into the experience itself. **Lesson: culture converts when action is easy, specific and physically present.** [REVERB impact report](https://reverb.org/impact_report/hit-me-hard-and-soft-the-tour/)

## Coldplay — publish a baseline, reduce first, disclose external review

Coldplay reports a 59% show-by-show reduction in direct tour emissions against its prior stadium tour, with analysis overseen by Hope Solutions and reviewed by a professor associated with MIT's Environmental Solutions Initiative. The artist discloses boundaries, including the exclusion of fan travel; this is not treated here as a published independent assurance opinion. **Lesson: credibility rises when the baseline, boundary, method and external review are visible.** [Coldplay Tour Emissions Update](https://www.coldplay.com/emissions-update/)

## Global Citizen — connect cultural attention to decision-makers and commitments

Global Citizen’s 2025 festival campaign reports 4.3 million organisation-counted actions and uses a year-long policy campaign, action-earned tickets and an annual cultural moment to press governments and companies for commitments. These are not necessarily unique people or outcomes, and commitments remain separate from delivery. **Lesson: the halo event works best as a pressure and accountability point inside a longer campaign.** [Global Citizen Festival 2025 Impact](https://www.globalcitizen.org/en/content/global-citizen-festival-2025-impact/)

## Provisional Rihanna / CLF strategic white space

This is directional, based on a selective comparator set rather than an exhaustive market audit. Rihanna should not imitate sustainable touring or a generic petition platform. Her differentiated territory is **community-led resilience in the Caribbean and U.S. South**, with culture used to reveal infrastructure, justice, leadership and finance that already exist.

# 09 / STRATEGIC TERRITORY

## REBUILD & REIMAGINE

### Promise

Climate justice is what happens when communities have the power and infrastructure to keep learning, healing and living through a changing climate.

### Line

**Built by communities. Backed by culture. Measured in resilience.**

### Four principles

1. **Community authority** — partners define need, solution, consent and story.
2. **Receipts before rhetoric** — every story connects to a named action and evidence status.
3. **Everyday resilience** — show value before, during and after a disaster.
4. **Culture as multiplier** — Rihanna’s role is to make verified work visible, desirable to support and hard to ignore.

### Narrative structure

- **What is at risk?** Schools, clinics, water, energy, livelihoods and cultural continuity.
- **Who leads?** Local and Indigenous organisations, public systems and frontline communities.
- **What changes?** Infrastructure, flexible finance, service continuity and decision power.
- **What can I do?** Learn, share accurately, give, volunteer, advocate, buy only where there is a verified cause-to-commerce mechanism, and act again.

### Message guardrail

Rihanna is not “giving communities a voice.” Communities already have voices. The platform gives those voices reach, resources and leverage.

### Alternatives considered—and why they are weaker

**A broad sustainability platform** would offer topical breadth but dilute Rihanna's founder–cause authenticity and duplicate territory already occupied by many artists and brands. It would also invite product-footprint claims that cannot be defended from the public evidence available here.

**A disaster-response-only platform** would be emotionally immediate but episodic. It risks turning Caribbean communities into scenes of crisis while obscuring the slower work of preparedness, infrastructure, health access, local finance and everyday continuity.

**A donation-led celebrity campaign** could move money quickly but would leave the central audience problem unresolved: whether people understand climate justice, recognise local leadership, return to act and support systemic change. Donation is one rung of the action ladder, not the organising idea.

**A product-first climate proposition** would meet audiences inside commerce but place the highest-evidence burden on the least mature part of the strategy. Without audited product, supply-chain and allocation data, it creates more reputational risk than strategic value. Commerce is therefore a gated pathway inside Rebuild & Reimagine—not the definition of the platform.

## Four connected strategic moves

### 1. Make the proof legible

Create a persistent Rebuild & Reimagine evidence hub that connects every public story to a place, partner, intervention, evidence status and next action.

### 2. Turn fandom into a return pathway

Build a tiered action journey that moves people from seeing and learning to verified action, return behaviour and partner-defined leadership. Fans may help direct attention or participate among partner-approved choices; they should not overrule frontline grant decisions.

### 3. Commission culture as infrastructure

Fund Caribbean creators, filmmakers, educators and cultural institutions to translate community-defined priorities into work with reach, memory and local value. The annual halo moment should be a public accountability checkpoint, not a one-night substitute for programme delivery.

This builds on an existing CLF premise: its Arts & Culture work explicitly treats creativity as a force for healing, resilience and systems change, and its 2025 report identifies amplification of creative voices as a forward priority. [CLF About](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/about); [CLF 2025 Annual Report](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/2025report)

### 4. Gate commerce behind proof

Explore a Fenty-facing evidence or traceability layer only after product, supply-chain and cause-to-commerce claims have auditable boundaries. Until then, treat Fenty participation as philanthropy, distribution or matched funding—not evidence of product sustainability.

### What must be true before launch

Rebuild & Reimagine should advance only when five conditions are met. First, the participating community or delivery partner has approved the need, proposed benefit, public narrative and consent process. Second, the intervention has a defined baseline and a result that can be measured without imposing disproportionate reporting work on the partner. Third, the audience pathway names a real action and a return moment; awareness alone is not presented as transformation. Fourth, any commercial contribution has a reconciled calculation, a transfer schedule and language that distinguishes revenue, profit, donation and programme expenditure. Fifth, the public evidence card identifies what is reported, what is independently verified, what remains provisional and who owns the underlying data.

These conditions turn the territory from a campaign idea into a governable strategic platform. They also create a clear **no-go** rule: if a launch depends on an environmental claim that cannot be audited, a community story that has not been consented, or an impact number that cannot be traced to an owner and period, it does not launch under the Rebuild & Reimagine name. Scarcity is preferable to weak proof. The platform earns cultural authority through the quality of what it can stand behind, not the volume of material it publishes.

# 12 / THE ACTION LADDER

## Attention is not the outcome. Design for progression.

### Level 1 — SEE

Watch a partner story; view a project evidence card; understand one climate-justice concept.

### Level 2 — LEARN

Complete a short learning sequence; identify why impacts and finance are unequal; choose a local or regional pathway.

### Level 3 — ACT

Donate, volunteer, contact a decision-maker, join a verified partner action or support an approved local programme.

### Level 4 — RETURN

Complete a second action in 30–90 days; receive a project update; invite another person into the pathway.

### Level 5 — LEAD

Host, fundraise, create, organise or advocate with a partner-defined brief.

### Quality rules

- An action must have a named beneficiary or decision target.
- A pledge is not counted as behaviour unless follow-through is observable.
- Donation, advocacy, volunteering and learning are reported separately.
- Repeat action matters more than total clicks.
- Negative, critical and dissenting responses remain in the analysis.

A field experiment involving 1,560 Vietnamese university-dorm participants found that information shifted attitudes, while a local-celebrity endorsement condition also affected single-use-plastic behaviour for approximately one month. This supports testing specific, observable behaviours rather than awareness alone. The population, culture, celebrity and behaviour differ materially from this strategy; the result is not a Rihanna or climate-justice forecast. [Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268122001226)

# 14 / PARTNERSHIP ARCHITECTURE

Partnership is the operating condition of the strategy, not a campaign embellishment. Climate resilience crosses infrastructure, health, education, utilities, finance, rights, culture and public policy. Rihanna and CLF should therefore act as a convening and amplification layer around organisations with delivery authority, technical expertise and lived knowledge. The system is strongest when each institution does what it is uniquely positioned to do and the programme makes those roles visible.

The governing design separates **authority**, **advice**, **delivery**, **amplification** and **capital**. Community and delivery partners define relevance, consent and benefit. Technical and measurement partners establish standards and evaluate evidence. Cultural partners translate priorities without appropriating them. Institutions and governments change systems or finance infrastructure. Commercial partners may fund, distribute or alter operations, but they do not acquire narrative ownership or beneficiary-selection power.

This architecture also protects small organisations from becoming unpaid evidence suppliers to a global campaign. Reporting requirements should be proportionate, co-designed and funded. Where CLF or Re-Model requires additional data for the dashboard, the budget should include partner time, local research capacity, translation, safeguarding and data stewardship.

## Community council

CLF grantees and Caribbean practitioners with authority over programme relevance, story consent and benefit.

## Measurement council

CLF, grantee data owners, an independent evaluator, resilience specialists and Re-Model Intelligence.

## Culture council

Caribbean artists, filmmakers, designers, educators and youth leaders—not only global celebrities.

## Institutional partners

Governments, schools, health systems, utilities, academia, disaster-risk and climate-finance organisations.

## Commercial partners

Brands may fund, distribute or change operations. They do not control beneficiary selection or make environmental claims beyond verified boundaries.

### Fenty boundary

Fenty Beauty, Fenty Skin and Savage X Fenty are listed as 2025 CLF anchor donors. This supports a real philanthropic relationship. This public review did not locate an assurance-ready Fenty-specific climate footprint or product baseline; that does not establish that no internal baseline exists. Any cause-to-commerce mechanism must be separately defined, audited and approved. [CLF 2025 Annual Report](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/2025report)

CLF's current website also states that 35% of selected Fenty product sales goes directly to the foundation. That is a real starting point for pipeline design, but the report would still need to establish eligible products, reporting period, gross-versus-net basis, climate allocation and proof of transfer before treating it as a climate-justice result. [CLF Home](https://www.claralionelfoundation.org/)

### Partnership selection test

The programme should select partners against six questions before prestige or reach is considered: **authority**—does the organisation have legitimacy with the people affected; **additionality**—does the partnership add capital, capability, access or policy leverage that would not otherwise exist; **evidence fitness**—can the intended result be documented at a proportionate cost; **rights and consent**—are story, image, data and withdrawal rights explicit; **continuity**—is there a plan beyond the public moment; and **independence**—can community partners speak honestly about limits or failure without risking support.

No single partner must satisfy every role. The architecture is intentionally plural: a grassroots organisation may hold community authority, a public agency may control infrastructure, a technical body may validate a standard, and a cultural collaborator may make the work legible. The programme office is responsible for making those dependencies clear and preventing brand visibility from being mistaken for delivery authority.

# 15 / IMPACT SCORECARD

The scorecard is a decision framework, not a single ranking. It keeps different forms of value visible long enough to prevent a strong cultural result from masking a weak programme result—or a strong programme result from being judged only by social reach. Resilience, justice, partner capacity, cultural position, audience action and commercial value may interact, but their evidence sources, time horizons and ethical stakes are different.

A future composite score should be considered only after the domains, components, weights, baselines and minimum evidence thresholds are approved. Until then, the dashboard should show a profile rather than a verdict. Missing data is a result in its own right and should remain visible.

## A. Resilience and service continuity

- days of education disruption after a shock;
- days of health-service disruption after a shock;
- facility uptime for power, water and communications;
- time to reopen or return to agreed service level;
- people served during normal operations and emergency periods;
- damaged or disrupted education and health facilities.

These align naturally with the Sendai Framework’s Target D, including damaged health and education facilities and disruptions to health, education and other basic services. [UNDRR Sendai monitoring](https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/monitoring-sendai-framework)

## B. Justice and community power

- percentage of climate funds governed or advised by local partners;
- percentage of funding that is flexible or unrestricted;
- funding to women-, youth-, Black-, Indigenous- and locally led organisations;
- partner reporting burden;
- partner satisfaction and psychological safety;
- number and quality of community decisions adopted;
- capital mobilised directly to partner organisations.

## C. Partner and system capacity

- organisations funded and retained;
- partner staff or systems strengthened;
- co-funding leverage with method disclosed;
- government or institutional commitments implemented;
- project maintenance and lifecycle financing secured.

## D. Cultural position

- aided and unaided Rihanna × climate-justice association;
- comprehension of the justice frame;
- partner-name recognition;
- trust and authenticity;
- share of relevant conversation, with a transparent query;
- quality of conversation coding;
- geographic and community diversity of visible voices.

## E. Audience action

- verified actions by type;
- action completion rate;
- qualified-action rate per 1,000 reached;
- repeat action in 30, 90 and 365 days;
- progression up the action ladder;
- partner-attributable funds or advocacy contacts.

## F. Commercial and career value

- relevance, trust and consideration among defined cohorts;
- earned media quality, not only volume;
- partner and talent opportunity created;
- attributed revenue only where a consented and auditable pathway exists;
- long-term career-equity indicators separated from social impact.

## Evaluation lenses

Use the OECD DAC criteria—relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact and sustainability—as questions, not as a mechanistic rating system. [OECD Evaluation Criteria](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/development-co-operation-evaluation-and-effectiveness/evaluation-criteria.html)

# 18 / RESEARCH DESIGN

The measured edition requires a staged research programme in which each method answers a different question. Public evidence establishes delivery credibility and the issue system. Owned analytics describe activity on authorised accounts. Licensed listening estimates defined public conversation within known source limits. Primary research tests awareness, comprehension, trust and action. Partner evidence addresses programme outcomes and community power. Analyst interpretation connects those lanes without pretending that any one of them represents the whole audience.

## Stakeholder validation panel

Before a measured strategy is approved, the research should be tested with at least ten distinct stakeholder perspectives: CLF executive leadership; programme and grantmaking teams; Caribbean delivery partners; community or youth representatives; resilience and infrastructure specialists; climate-finance or policy experts; cultural creators; Rihanna's communications team; Fenty or commercial representatives where relevant; and an independent measurement or ethics adviser. This is a proposed validation architecture. No stakeholder interviews were conducted for this public-evidence edition.

The purpose of the panel is not consensus for its own sake. It is to expose where the same proposition carries different risks, where a metric creates burden, where a cultural idea loses meaning and where decision rights are unclear. Findings should record convergence, disagreement and unresolved trade-offs rather than smoothing them into a single institutional voice.

## Phase 0 — public-evidence foundation

# 19 / DIGITAL-TWIN STRATEGY TESTING

## Test the strategy before it spends cultural capital

Re-Model proposes a human-validated digital-twin layer between desk research and live activation. The purpose is not to manufacture audience truth. It is to expose weak assumptions, compare content and strategy variants consistently, and decide what is worth advancing to real people.

### The five-gate loop

1. **GROUND** — define the decision, population and relevant evidence using authorised analytics, permissioned research, partner knowledge and public context.
2. **MODEL** — build or select cohorts by relevant attributes such as age, geography, life stage, values and psychographics, subject to rights, ethics, sufficiency and bias review.
3. **STRESS-TEST** — hold the cohort constant while varying message, spokesperson, visual, format, call-to-action, sequencing and frequency.
4. **VALIDATE** — take leading variants and predicted failure modes into representative human research, partner review and limited live experiments.
5. **LEARN** — compare prediction with observation, disclose uncertainty and update or reject the model.

### MatrAIx route — open research infrastructure

MatrAIx describes itself as population-scale simulated-user evaluation for AI systems and digital products across survey, chatbot, web and app environments. Its 2026 preprint reports 8.3 billion persona records across 1,290 dimensions, a public coreset of approximately one million personas and 91.5% declared-behaviour adherence in a 400-trial validation study. The public dataset card also states that the coreset is not representative of any population and contains both human-grounded and synthetic records. [MatrAIx paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04205); [MatrAIx Persona 1M](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MatrAIx2026/MatrAIx_Persona_1M)

The work is organised by a large research collaboration whose corresponding organisers use Harvard and MIT affiliations. It should be described as **Harvard- and MIT-affiliated research**, not as an official Harvard or MIT product.

### Simile route — bespoke enterprise simulation

Simile is a separate Stanford-founded company built on generative-agent and interview-grounded population-simulation research. Its official methodology says populations begin with real people, may incorporate opt-in customer-governed behavioural and transactional data, and are repeatedly validated against human responses. Those operating and customer claims are vendor-reported and require procurement and diligence. [Simile methodology](https://www.simile.com/); [Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)

### Strategy and content tests

- comprehension of climate-justice language;
- authenticity and role fit across Rihanna, CLF and partners;
- relative appeal of proof stories, formats and calls to action;
- confusion, backlash, cause-washing and community-eclipse risks;
- action-ladder fit by market and cohort;
- frequency, sequencing and attention-decay scenarios;
- robustness of the Commercial-to-Climate Pipeline before real-world investment.

### Non-negotiable boundary

Digital twins are decision-support systems. They do not replace representative people, partner consent, lived experience or real-world validation. Re-Model should never publish simulated outputs as Rihanna audience measurements, train on private fan or client data without rights, or use synthetic personas to infer sensitive traits. Every study should disclose population, source, model, calibration, uncertainty, decision rule and validation date.

# 22 / THE RE-MODEL IMPACT OPERATING SYSTEM

## The report defines the strategic contract. The dashboard keeps it alive.

The Re-Model methodology is not a sequence of disconnected deliverables. It is a learning system. **RE-SEARCH** establishes the evidence and the boundaries of what can be claimed. **RE-POSITION** converts that evidence into a distinctive role, a target state and a scorecard. **RE-CREATE** translates the position into partnerships, content, commerce and participation. **RE-ACTION + RE-ITERATE** measures what changed, exposes weak assumptions and determines the next cycle.

For Rihanna and CLF, that system prevents two common failures. The first is a compelling cultural campaign detached from programme truth. The second is strong delivery evidence that never becomes understandable or actionable to a wider public. The report connects the two by making each recommendation answerable to a source, a decision owner, a causal assumption and a future metric.

### How the report connects to Talent / Audience / Target

| Intelligence layer | What this report establishes | What the dashboard monitors | Decision enabled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Talent / CLF** | delivery credibility, founder–cause authenticity, narrative strengths and weaknesses | evidence freshness, proof coverage, narrative coherence and public association | what Rihanna and CLF can credibly lead or claim |
| **Audience** | the hypotheses, cohorts, questions and research design required for a baseline | awareness, comprehension, trust, action, return and segment differences | which message, format and pathway should be advanced or changed |
| **Target** | the desired position, impact thresholds and guardrails | distance to the approved state, confidence and evidence gaps | whether the strategy is progressing or merely generating activity |

### The annual intelligence rhythm

The dashboard should not become a real-time theatre of fluctuating sentiment. Climate-justice strategy requires different cadences for different evidence. Owned-channel and listening signals may refresh monthly. Audience comprehension and trust are better read through quarterly pulse research. Infrastructure and partner outcomes should follow the reporting rhythm that is proportionate and useful to delivery organisations. Strategic interpretation belongs in a quarterly analyst review, while the annual accountability publication reconciles results, limitations and next-cycle decisions.

Every dashboard value therefore carries a signal-health record: definition, provenance, owner, source, period, geography, population, denominator, confidence and missingness. If any of those elements fail, the visual should degrade visibly rather than present false precision. This makes the dashboard a governance interface as much as a communications product.

### The Re-Model value chain

**Evidence → position → cultural expression → participation → observed result → interpretation → redesign**

The value is not the volume of graphics or the frequency of reporting. It is the disciplined conversion of diverse signals into a smaller number of defensible decisions: what to say, who should say it, what action to ask for, which partner should own it, what evidence is sufficient and when to stop or change course.

# 24 / METHOD

## How the report was built

The analytical structure follows Re-Model Intelligence's four-phase impact operating system:

1. **RE-SEARCH** — commercial, customer and social evidence; publishable and internal outputs.
2. **RE-POSITION** — unique position, message system, partner map and customised scorecard.
3. **RE-CREATE** — always-on content, partnerships and annual halo moment.
4. **RE-ACTION + RE-ITERATE** — scorecard, dashboard, learning and extension.

The four-phase process governs how evidence becomes position, activation and accountable learning. The RMU dashboard is the living implementation layer; its current display values are explicitly illustrative until the baseline, components and weights are approved.

## Research hierarchy

Priority was given to:

1. CLF’s current reports and programme pages;
2. public financial records;
3. authoritative multilateral climate and resilience sources;
4. peer-reviewed celebrity-advocacy research;
5. official comparator impact reports;
6. current official platform and vendor access policies.

## Limitations

- No private data or interviews were available.
- CLF programme results are reported by CLF unless otherwise stated.
- The public evidence review does not constitute audit or assurance.
- Social and search observations are not a representative audience baseline.
- The strategy is a recommendation requiring partner and client validation.
- Platform and vendor access terms can change; procurement should re-check them.
